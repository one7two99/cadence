L4 Symbol Layer

Design rationale, key placement, and development history. Specification shared by Cadence (Ferris Sweep, 34 keys) and Sonata (28 keys).

1. Overview

L4 is the most frequently used non-base layer. It is activated by holding Bsp (right thumb, outer key) and carries 21 symbols across both hands and the left thumb cluster. The layout targets mixed German + English + code workloads.

This specification establishes a shared L4 layout across the Cadence (Sweep) and Sonata (28-key) sister projects. Cadenza retains its v1.0.0 heritage layout for muscle-memory continuity with users of the Corne Choc daily driver.

2. Design principles

Access asymmetry

L4 is activated by holding the right thumb's outer key (Bsp). The right hand is therefore anchored during typing, and the right bottom row becomes the layout's worst region — middle and ring fingers must work against the thumb anchor to reach it. High-frequency symbols stay on the freer left hand or on the upper rows of the right hand. The right bottom row is left transparent.

Frequency × strength

Symbols ranked by daily usage frequency are assigned to fingers in strength order, weighted by the position-quality penalty for the anchored right hand. Index ranks higher than middle, middle higher than ring, ring higher than pinky.

No inner column for layer content

G, V, B (left) and J, M, K (right) require an inward index stretch. They never carry layer content — only their letters and App/Menu on hold. This rule holds without exception.

Tap dance for low-cost redundancy

A tap-or-hold tap dance with no double-tap action commits the tap on key release without latency. Latency only appears when a double-tap action is also assigned, requiring the firmware to wait for a possible second tap. With this constraint understood, tap dances become viable for low-cost redundancy: pairing a primary high-frequency symbol with a low-frequency secondary on the same strong position. The primary input pays no speed penalty; only the secondary requires an explicit hold gesture.

Cross-family consistency

Cadence and Sonata share this exact L4 specification. A user who switches between the Sweep and the 28-key Sonata maintains identical symbol muscle memory. Cadenza is treated as heritage and retains its v1.0.0 layout.

5. Development process

The redesign progressed through six conceptual stages. Each stage resolved a problem the previous step had exposed.

1

Identifying the access-asymmetry problem

The starting point was Cadence v1.5.0 / v1.8.0, which placed ?, :, and % on the right bottom row. Daily use revealed these positions as painful: when L4 is activated by holding Bsp, the right thumb is anchored, and the right hand's middle and ring fingers must work against this anchor to reach the bottom row.

The "Frequency × Strength" principle alone proved insufficient — it ignored a layer-specific access asymmetry created by single-thumb activation. The corrective principle: under an asymmetric layer trigger, the anchored hand's bottom row is the layout's worst region, regardless of which finger reaches it.

2

Relieving the right bottom row

The first move relocated ? and : off the anchored bottom row. ? went to a top-row position (W, ring top), : to ring bottom on the free left hand (X). Both gained unanchored-hand access. Ring finger is strong enough for mid-frequency symbols. % remained on the right side temporarily, awaiting a better destination.

3

Thumb cluster freed for symbol use

A subsequent layer-architecture refactor freed the left thumb keys (Spc, Tab) from layer-trigger duties. These positions are first-tier ergonomic real estate: the thumb operates as a single-joint action that does not require any finger to leave its home position.

They became the natural home for - (Spc, inner thumb) and = (Tab, outer thumb), removing pressure from the left hand letter rows.

4

Tap dance latency clarified

An initial concern was that adding tap dances to high-frequency keys would slow them down. On closer technical review, this proved overly broad: a tap-or-hold tap dance with no double-tap action commits the tap on key release without waiting for a tapping-term-style timeout. Latency only appears when a double-tap action is also assigned, requiring the firmware to wait for a possible second tap before committing the single-tap.

With this constraint understood, tap dances became viable for low-cost redundancy: pairing a primary high-frequency symbol with a low-frequency secondary on the same strong position. The primary symbol pays no speed penalty; only the secondary requires the explicit hold gesture.

5

Final optimizations and completeness check

Six tap dances and one direct relocation completed the right bottom row's clearance. A subsequent completeness check against the standard US-keyboard symbol set surfaced two missing symbols (&, ^); both were added as TD-holds, following the same hold-on-strong-position pattern as the other tap dances:

  • T (left index home) — tap = *, hold = @. Frees the W-position previously held by @, used now for ?.
  • S (left middle home) — tap = !, hold = &. Added during the completeness check.
  • D (left index bot) — tap = /, hold = \. \ remains primary on L9; the L4 hold is redundant for evaluation.
  • A (left pinky home) — direct keycode %. The last anchor-zone symbol relocates to home row on the free hand.
  • N (right index home) — tap = ., hold = (. Brings the most-used opening parenthesis to home row.
  • E (right middle home) — tap = ,, hold = ). Pairs with N for closing parenthesis.
  • I (right ring home) — tap = ', hold = ^. Added during the completeness check; preserves the Z-position as a reserve slot rather than spending it on a low-frequency direct binding.

The right bottom row is entirely free of bindings. All standard US-keyboard symbols are reachable on L4, with two intentionally placed elsewhere (see §6).

6

Cross-family alignment

The resulting specification is adopted as the shared L4 layout across Cadence (Sweep) and Sonata (28-key). Cadenza retains its v1.0.0 heritage layout for muscle-memory continuity with the Corne Choc daily driver.

This intentional split: Cadence and Sonata are spec-shared sister projects evolving together; Cadenza is heritage, anchored to its earlier design.

6. Cross-layer notes

Two symbols are deliberately not exclusive to L4:

  • \ — primary placement on L9 (Code & CLI), where it conceptually belongs alongside other code-specific operators (||, &&, =>, etc.). Also reachable on L4 as the hold of the D-position TD (tap = /, hold = \). The redundancy is deliberate; sustained use will reveal whether the L9 access or the L4 hold feels more natural. Either may be removed in a future patch if the other proves unused.
  • " — placed on L_INTL (International Layer), not on L4. The L_INTL layer carries paired typographic forms together with the umlaut dead keys, keeping all "-related logic in one place. L4's I-position therefore carries only the apostrophe (').
